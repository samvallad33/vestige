# Structurally Unstealable: 2026 Cognitive-Infrastructure Moats

## 1. What this document is

This is the third and highest-bar research pass on Vestige's competitive position, following the exploratory frontier scan (`docs/research/COGNITIVE-INFRASTRUCTURE-FRONTIER-2026.md`). The first two passes asked "what's underdeveloped" and "what's novel." This pass asks a harder question: what capability, if Vestige builds it, could a well-funded competitor NOT cheaply replicate.

The process: 17 ideation agents ran in parallel, 7 assigned cross-disciplinary lenses (immunology, market microstructure, distributed systems, cognitive neuroscience, cryptography/provenance, control theory, and legal/audit doctrine) and 10 assigned forced-combination briefs pairing two or more of Vestige's real architectural primitives (typed causal edges, retroactive salience backfill, the Fellegi-Sunter merge/supersede scorer, the Active/Dormant/Silent/Unavailable memory-state continuum, receipts, portable merge/import, the single MCP dispatch chokepoint, and others) that had never been composed together. That produced 58 raw candidates.

Those 58 were triaged down to the 9 strongest, each of which was then run through four independent, sequential checks:

1. **Novelty**: multi-query web and literature search to confirm no shipped product or published 2026 paper already does this exact thing, not merely something adjacent to it.
2. **Buildability**: grounded against the actual Vestige source (not the pitch, not memory, not the deleted `.council/` planning docs) to confirm the primitives it claims to fuse are real, wired, and load-bearing in the way the mechanism requires.
3. **Red-team A**: an adversarial pass whose only job was to find the cheapest possible replication a well-funded competitor could ship in about one sprint, and determine whether that replication reaches 80%+ of the user-visible value.
4. **Red-team B**: a second, independently-run adversarial pass with the same mandate, run separately so a mechanism can't survive by getting lucky with one red-team's blind spot.

A candidate had to clear all four checks to count as a defensible moat. **Zero candidates cleared all four.** That is the headline finding of this pass, and it is reported straight, not softened: the honest output of rigorous adversarial testing was that every mechanism we could think of either already exists somewhere in the 2026 literature, doesn't actually fuse the primitives it claims to, or can be approximated to a user's satisfaction by a competitor with one sprint and infrastructure they already operate.

This is not a failure of the process. It is what the process is for. A moat-hunt that finds nine moats is a moat-hunt that wasn't trying hard enough to break its own candidates. Section 2 explains what "surviving" would have required and why none of the nine got there, with emphasis on the two closest calls. Section 3 gives the full failure record for all nine, because the rigor of a null result is only credible if the reasoning is visible. Section 4 covers what to build next given this outcome.

## 2. Surviving mechanisms

None. Every one of the nine shortlisted candidates was cracked by at least one of the two red-teams, ruled not novel, or found not buildable as described. There is no ranked list of surviving moats to present here, and padding this section with a "closest we got" writeup framed as a near-win would misrepresent the result.

What is worth stating plainly, because it changes what Sam should do next, is the shape of the near-misses. Two of the nine candidates split their red-team verdicts, meaning one red-team found a true moat and the other found a cheap crack. That is the strongest signal this pass produced:

- **Fleet Causal-Outcome Arbitration & Reputation** cleared novelty, cleared buildability, and Red-Team B concluded it was a genuine moat requiring a competitor to rebuild their ingest pipeline around non-collapsing concurrent writers plus unbounded multi-hop causal traversal. Red-Team A found a graph-native competitor (Zep/Graphiti specifically) could reach 80% of the value with a bounded-window LLM-judge bolt-on. The mechanism is real and the gap it targets is real; it did not survive because one adversarial reviewer found a crack the other one didn't rule out.
- **Causal Git Archaeology** cleared novelty and buildability, and Red-Team A concluded the three-primitive fusion (typed causal edges spanning memory and commit nodes, a deterministic multi-hop backward trace, and real git-history classification) forces a structural rewrite for every named competitor. Red-Team B found that a single new `touched_files` metadata field plus a recursive git-log join reproduces the deep, cross-modal root-cause case the mechanism is built to showcase, without adopting any of Vestige's graph architecture.

Both of these are documented in full in Section 3, because "split verdict" is functionally the same outcome as "cracked" under this pass's rule (all four checks must pass, unanimously, to count as surviving). They are, however, the two places worth revisiting first if Vestige wants to re-run this pass after hardening the implementation, since they are one red-team finding away from clearing the bar.

The other seven candidates were cracked more decisively: two failed on novelty alone (functionally equivalent mechanisms already published in the 2026 literature), two failed on buildability (the primitives the mechanism claims to fuse are not actually wired the way the pitch describes), and three were cracked by both red-teams independently.

## 3. Investigated but not defensible

All nine shortlisted candidates, in the order they were evaluated. Each entry states the mechanism, the combination it claimed to fuse, and which check(s) killed it.

### 3.1 Fleet Causal-Outcome Arbitration & Reputation
**Combination claimed:** typed causal edges (P1) + deferred/retroactive backward traversal (P2) + a concurrent multi-writer graph that does not collapse contested claims at write time (P22) + trust-weighted contradiction detection (P13) + receipts (P3), fused into a system where contradictory claims from different fleet agents are left Pending and resolved retroactively, hours or days later, by an unrelated writer's causally-linked outcome event, with the result folded into a persistent Brier-scored per-writer reputation.

**Killed by:** split red-team verdict, counted as cracked. Novelty and buildability both passed: the mechanism is not published anywhere else in the 2026 literature, and the real Vestige codebase does have `EdgeType::Causal`, a working `retroactive_backfill.rs` autofire path wired into consolidation, and a `memory_prs` pending-review table that is a genuine precedent for a Contested state (though it is not currently wired to contradiction detection). Red-Team B found the mechanism a true moat for the general, unbounded-lag, cross-session, multi-hop case, arguing that most competitor ingest pipelines resolve conflicts synchronously at write time by design, so keeping two live contested claims around for arbitrary future resolution is a pipeline change, not a bolt-on. Red-Team A disagreed for the single most dangerous competitor archetype: Zep/Graphiti already ships typed, bi-temporal edges and LLM-based contradiction detection as deeply integrated primitives, so extending "resolve immediately" to "defer, then backward-trace on a later outcome, then score writer reputation" is weeks of feature work on an existing substrate, not an architectural rewrite. Because the bar requires holding against a well-funded competitor, and at least one such competitor's existing architecture gets most of the way there via feature-add, the mechanism does not clear.

### 3.2 Causal Git Archaeology
**Combination claimed:** typed causal edges (P1) + git commit mining and classification (P12) + a backward multi-hop traversal that treats commit nodes and memory nodes as interchangeable graph members, so a root-cause trace for a live failure can walk back through both real git history and conversational decisions that were never committed as text, surfacing an ancestor that is many hops removed and semantically dissimilar to the failure.

**Killed by:** split red-team verdict, counted as cracked. Novelty passed (no competitor or paper unifies git history and a memory causal graph into one traversable structure with auto-linked typed edges). Buildability passed with corrections: the typed causal edge is real, and a live multi-hop traversal engine exists in `chains.rs` (though the build sketch pointed at the wrong module, `retroactive_backfill.rs`, which is single-hop by design), and git-mining code (`GitAnalyzer`) is real but currently dead code with zero callers from the live MCP server. Red-Team A found this a true moat: no competitor currently treats git commits as first-class nodes in a causal graph with typed edges, and faking the flagship case (an ancestor several hops back, never restated in a commit message, semantically unlike the failure) requires exactly the persisted, deterministic, re-traversable structure the mechanism builds. Red-Team B cracked it: a single new `touched_files` metadata field on memories, joined against `git log`/`git blame` output by file-and-time-window overlap, recovers the same "semantically dissimilar, never committed as text" case, because the join key is structural (files and time), not semantic, and is exactly the join key Vestige's own mechanism uses to decide where to write its edges in the first place. For a graph-native competitor this is a feature add on infrastructure they already ship, not a rewrite.

### 3.3 Causal-Fidelity-Gated MDL Consolidation ("Rate-Distortion Dream Cycle")
**Combination claimed:** typed causal edges (P1) + retroactive backfill (P2) + the Fellegi-Sunter merge scorer (P6) + a scheduled dream/consolidation cycle (P14), fused so that a proposed memory merge is rejected or downgraded if a deterministic pre/post root-cause backfill query over the causal subgraph would change its answer, with an accepted-rate and bits-saved receipt logged per cycle.

**Killed by:** buildability. The individual primitives exist in the codebase, but the specific integration point the mechanism describes does not. `ConsolidationScheduler` itself is live in production, wired into `vestige-mcp/src/cognitive.rs` and gating the inline consolidation trigger in `server.rs`, but only through its `should_consolidate()` method. Its `run_consolidation_cycle` method, the one the build sketch says to extend, has zero production callers, referenced only by its own doc-comment and one unit test. The merge path that actually runs unattended, `auto_dedup_consolidation` inside `Storage::run_consolidation()`, is a separate, simpler cosine-threshold merge-and-delete routine that never calls the Fellegi-Sunter scorer and, by its own code comment, "HARD-DELETES the weaker ones with no reflog." There is also no MDL, compression, or bits-saved concept anywhere in the codebase; it would need to be invented from nothing. This is buildable as new architecture, but not as the "wire together existing primitives" change it was pitched as, and both red-teams independently concluded that a shallow LLM-judge pre/post check bolted onto any existing summarization pipeline reproduces the visible outcome for the common, shallow-chain case, which is the majority of real consolidation events.

### 3.4 Causal Propagation of Staleness (forward Retroactive Salience Backfill)
**Combination claimed:** the same typed causal edges (P1) and backward traversal (P2) used by Retroactive Salience Backfill, run in reverse: a forward walk from a newly-written fact through causal edges to auto-discover and demote (Active to Dormant to Silent) memories that are now implicitly stale, with a receipt naming the causal chain, reversibly.

**Killed by:** novelty. This is not a new mechanism. MEMOREPAIR (arXiv 2605.07242) already formalizes the identical "cascade update problem": forward propagation from an invalidated or updated source through dependency edges to descendant memories, removal from active service before repair, and reversible, receipt-like republication gated on validated predecessors. STALE (arXiv 2605.06527) independently names and benchmarks the same failure class as "Type II propagated invalidation across structurally dependent attributes," with a prototype that forward-searches for structurally affected state at write time. The proposer's own competitor check only covered shipped products (mem0, Zep, Letta) and missed this directly on-point concurrent research. Buildability was also mixed (the codebase's real multi-hop traversal engine lives in `spreading_activation.rs`, not the backward-only `retroactive_backfill.rs` the build sketch named), and both red-teams found a one-sprint LLM-judge-plus-status-field version reproduces the common case, but novelty is the disqualifying check here since a close functional equivalent already exists in the published literature.

### 3.5 Antibody Memories: Affinity Maturation via Clonal Selection on Root-Cause Explanations
**Combination claimed:** causal edges (P1) + retroactive backfill (P2) + Fellegi-Sunter merge/supersede scoring (P6) + receipts (P3) + trust-weighted contradiction detection (P13), fused so that competing root-cause explanations for the same recurring failure re-compete over time as new occurrences arrive, with the higher-precision explanation superseding as the current "generation" and losers demoted into a visible, inspectable lineage rather than deleted.

**Killed by:** both red-teams (novelty check was not completed for this candidate). Both red-team passes converged on the same crack independently: three of the five claimed primitives have generic, already-shipping substitutes. Fellegi-Sunter merge scoring is replaceable with a single LLM-judge call, which is arguably a more natural tool for ranking explanation quality than a probabilistic entity-resolution scorer. Trust-weighted contradiction detection is replaceable with a plain success/failure counter with decay. Receipts are replaceable with a standard audit-log row. The one primitive with a real hard-to-fake core, causal backfill for stable root-cause identity, is itself trivially fake-able for the large share of real-world failures that are crash-shaped, since deterministic stack-trace fingerprinting (the Sentry/Rollbar approach, over a decade old) already solves "same root cause" for that case. A hybrid of one real technique plus three generic bolt-ons gets a demo-plausible, moderately durable result without adopting Vestige's architecture.

### 3.6 Authority-Aware Provenance Cascades
**Combination claimed:** an authority tier attached to every memory's provenance (P3) that survives multi-hop consolidation by always propagating as the minimum, never the maximum, of its inputs when the Fellegi-Sunter merge scorer (P6) combines two records, plus a tombstone/lineage graph (P5) walked forward from a revoked or downgraded source to flag every derived summary as needing reverification.

**Killed by:** novelty. Two independent 2026 papers together already cover the whole claimed mechanism. AuthMem-Bench (arXiv 2608.01679) is built entirely around "authority collapse," the exact min-not-max propagation failure this candidate targets, and found it across 48 of 49 tested consolidator-and-backbone combinations. MemIR (arXiv 2605.25869) independently proposes a typed representation that keeps raw evidence, retrieval cues, and truth-bearing claims separated specifically so authorization cannot drift upward during a merge. Buildability also found the proposal's named integration point wrong (the real provenance struct is `SourceEnvelope` on `KnowledgeNode`, not the `Receipt` struct the build sketch named), and both red-teams separately concluded that min-propagation is a stateless wrapper function applicable to any merge algorithm regardless of sophistication, so it does not require Fellegi-Sunter specifically, and that a flattened transitive ancestor-ID array reproduces the forward-flagging behavior without needing true lineage-graph machinery.

### 3.7 Cross-Checkable Forgetting Receipt
**Combination claimed:** every suppression or purge tombstone (P5) carries the live numeric inputs the retrieval engine actually used to rank and decay that memory, the four `ImportanceSignals` channels (P11), the SIF retrieval penalty (P10), and the per-neighbor Rac1 cascade fan-out, rather than a text rationale, so the forgetting decision can be replayed and verified against production math instead of trusted as a post-hoc excuse.

**Killed by:** both red-teams. Novelty and buildability both passed (this is genuinely new relative to the 2026 literature, and the real Vestige codebase does have all four claimed primitives, though the importance channels are not currently wired into live per-query retrieval ranking, only encoding and offline consolidation, which both red-teams flagged as a mismatch between the pitch and the current architecture). Both red-team passes converged on the same crack: the headline user-visible promise, "a real number, not a fabricated excuse," does not actually require four independently-tracked psychological channels. Any competitor's existing scoring function, however simple, already produces some numeric ranking signal; logging that number into the tombstone instead of discarding it reproduces the "verifiable, not rationalized" claim for a fraction of the engineering cost. The richer, multi-dimensional, cascade-aware version is a real depth advantage, but it reads to users as a richer receipt, not as the difference between a real and a fake one.

### 3.8 Independent Corroboration vs. Copy-Provenance Detection
**Combination claimed:** at Vestige's portable merge/import path (P21), each incoming record's hash-chained receipt lineage (P3) is compared against the target store's existing lineage and classified as Identical, Derived-Edit, Independent-Convergent (raises trust, since the fleet's genuinely independent, non-live-synced local stores (P22) make a missing common ancestor real evidence of separate arrival), or Independent-Contradictory (routed to arbitration).

**Killed by:** both red-teams. Novelty passed cleanly (the closest paper, Mesh Memory Protocol, implements only a binary echo-detection primitive this depends on and explicitly names the fuller classification as open work it does not solve). Buildability found the hash-chaining itself does not exist anywhere in the codebase today and would need to be built from nothing, and that receipts are currently excluded from the portable-archive tables entirely. Both red-teams independently made the same structural point: the claimed primitives are scaffolding Vestige needs because of its own decentralized, portable-by-default architecture, not scaffolding a competitor needs to reach the same user-visible outcome. A centralized competitor (which is what most named competitors already are) never needs a merge/import event at all, because every write already lands in one shared table; multi-author convergence is representable trivially via an existing `author_id` field plus a nightly embedding-clustering job, no decentralization, portability, or cryptographic chaining required.

### 3.9 Ambient Free-Energy-Gated Intention Firing
**Combination claimed:** conditional intentions (P18) fire mid-turn, piggybacked on whatever Vestige tool call is already in flight from any connected agent framework, using a persistent per-intention belief state (P17, the codebase's "Free Energy Principle" predictive-retrieval module) updated incrementally at the single shared MCP dispatch chokepoint (P20) and firing only once accumulated prediction-error crosses a threshold, at zero new calls.

**Killed by:** buildability, and reinforced by both red-teams. The load-bearing primitive in the pitch, P17, does not exist as described: `predictive_retrieval.rs` is titled after the Free Energy Principle in its doc comments only, but the actual code (`PredictiveMemory` in `crates/vestige-core/src/neuroscience/predictive_retrieval.rs`) is a weighted union of four separate prediction sources, interest, temporal, co-access, and session, held in a non-persisted, in-memory `UserModel`, with zero embeddings, zero cosine similarity, and no relationship to the `Intention` type at all. The live, actually-dispatched intention tool (`crates/vestige-mcp/src/tools/intention_unified.rs`) also does not call `predictive_retrieval` at all; trigger evaluation runs through a separate module, `prospective_memory.rs`'s own pattern-matching engine (`ContextPattern::matches`, `check_triggers`), against the `Storage` trait, not against P17's free-energy accumulator. On top of that, the stdio-per-process transport means a true cross-agent "ambient" belief state would require a new database write on every single dispatch across the whole system to be visible cross-session, which contradicts the "zero new calls, near-free" framing the mechanism is sold on. Both red-teams separately identified the same practical substitute regardless of the architecture gap: an embedding-similarity exponential-moving-average accumulator, sitting inside any centralized memory API's existing per-call middleware (which two of the five named competitors already expose via their own MCP servers), reproduces paraphrase-robust, threshold-gated, multi-signal firing without needing free-energy math specifically.

## 4. Sequencing: what this means for the roadmap

Because zero candidates from this pass survived, there is no ranked moat backlog to sequence into the existing roadmap. What this pass does produce is a dependency map worth carrying into the next research or build cycle, since the two closest-to-surviving candidates (3.1 and 3.2) and several of the clearly-cracked ones failed in ways that point at the same underlying gap: real primitives that exist in the codebase but are not wired into the live path the way the roadmap in `docs/research/COGNITIVE-INFRASTRUCTURE-FRONTIER-2026.md` assumes.

**Blocked on the existing roadmap's build order, not independently actionable:**

- **Causal Git Archaeology (3.2)** depends on the codebase-awareness module (`GitAnalyzer`/`CodebaseMemory`) being wired into the live, storage-backed MCP path. It is currently real, tested code with zero callers from `vestige-mcp` today, disconnected exactly the way the frontier roadmap already flags codebase-awareness as dead weight. Reviving Causal Git Archaeology as a build target should follow, not precede, that roadmap's plan to wire codebase-awareness into production, since building the mechanism on top of the dormant subsystem would just be reviving a second orphaned module instead of fixing the first one.
- **Fleet Causal-Outcome Arbitration & Reputation (3.1)** depends on a writer/agent identity primitive (P22) that does not exist anywhere in the schema today; `source` is a loose free-text field, not a structured identity. This is not something the frontier roadmap currently scopes at all; if Vestige wants to re-attempt this candidate, writer identity and a persisted reputation table are new foundational work that should be scoped and sequenced before the arbitration logic itself, not bundled into the same PR.
- **Causal-Fidelity-Gated MDL Consolidation (3.3)** depends on retargeting the live automatic-merge path (`auto_dedup_consolidation`) to route through the Fellegi-Sunter scorer instead of its own cosine-threshold logic. That retarget is a prerequisite for any causal-fidelity gate to have something real to gate, and it is closer to a correctness fix than a moat feature; it is worth doing on its own merits regardless of whether the fuller mechanism is ever revisited.
- **Cross-Checkable Forgetting Receipt (3.7)** depends on wiring the four `ImportanceSignals` channels into live per-query retrieval ranking, which today only runs at encoding time and in offline consolidation. If synaptic-tagging or hippocampal-indexing work from the frontier roadmap ends up touching the live ranking path, that is the natural place to close this gap as a side effect rather than a dedicated project.

**Independent of the roadmap, could start immediately if Sam wants to re-attempt closing either near-miss:**

- Closing the gap in **3.1** against Red-Team A's specific crack (a graph-native competitor extending write-time resolution to deferred resolution as a feature, not a rewrite) does not require new Vestige infrastructure beyond the writer-identity work above; it requires deciding whether the differentiator is worth defending as "deferred, cross-session, causally-traced arbitration" specifically, which is a product-positioning question, not an engineering-sequencing one.
- Closing the gap in **3.2** against Red-Team B's specific crack (a `touched_files`-plus-time-window join reproducing the deep case without a persisted graph) is partly a positioning question and partly real engineering: the honest fix is to make the persisted, re-traversable, deterministic version of the mechanism cheap enough to build that it stops mattering whether a competitor's live-join hack is theoretically possible, since Vestige's version would be strictly more reliable and auditable for the same or lower cost once codebase-awareness is wired in anyway.

Recommendation: do not chase either near-miss as a standalone project right now. Both are one wiring decision away from a re-run, and that wiring decision (codebase-awareness live-path integration) is already the frontier roadmap's job, not a new moat-hunt's job. Re-run this pass on 3.1 and 3.2 specifically once codebase-awareness and a minimal writer-identity primitive exist, rather than building either moat mechanism on top of infrastructure this pass just confirmed is dormant.
