# Shard 8 algorithms

Ninety designs, S8-001 through S8-090. Forty are single mechanisms. Fifty are joint integer rules. Each joint rule is the conjunction of two mechanisms already searched as a pair: the combination seats a turn or a session that neither parent rule would seat on its own. Base `e2ecd1da037974c1835f563ad8b046f4a4ca3629`. Hook file `crates/vestige-core/src/retrieval.rs`.

Deterministic integer and ordinal arithmetic only. No body reads. No `rsb_seat_behind` edits. No `HYPERNYM_LATTICE` edits. No `rungs.rs` edits. No change to `max_per_session_top5`. Speaker is not a branch. The only lexical material is closed-class grammar (ordinals, next/then, negators) and orthographic shape (digit runs, midcaps, clause counts).

### S8-001 Ledger folio pick

Ledger folio pick (1494 (Pacioli); medieval charge books; accounting). A merchant folio is the page that carries the largest unpaid balance, not the first line the clerk touched. Among the fifty dump-pool turns (seated first, then t_scene>0), slot 1 becomes the turn whose exact query-group mask has the greatest popcount. Ties break by the integer bits of t_own, then by the lower turn index. The rule reads index masks only. It does not multiply BM25, scene, or any existing knob, and it does not look at ix.turns[ti].body. Math: `folio(t)=popcount(exact_mask(t)); slot1=argmax (folio, t_own.to_bits(), -t)`. ### S8-002 Dead-reckoning leg

Dead-reckoning leg (traverse board, 16th-19th c.; navigation). Dead reckoning advances a position by summing legs, and the ordinal asked for is a leg index, not a date. On an event-sequence query, or when the query contains a closed-class ordinal (first, second, third, last, next), every positive-scene turn in a session that intersects the query mask gets leg length equal to its first digit-run value, or 1 when it has no digit. The cumulative sum selects the leg. Last selects the final leg. The chosen turn, already scored, takes slot 1. No before/after partition and no edit to the event-sequence per-session cap.
